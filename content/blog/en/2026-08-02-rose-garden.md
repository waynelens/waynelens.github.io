## Introduction

I first heard about Rose Garden around the same time I learned about Elephant Trunk Rock as a dive site.<br>
But hardly anyone organized trips there, and the few plans that did come up always fell through.

Last week, I met up with [7p_hhh](https://www.instagram.com/7p_hhh/), [shaneliu_eternal1713](https://www.instagram.com/shaneliu_eternal1713/), and [_h_ven](https://www.instagram.com/_h_ven/)—friends I met on a Green Island dive trip last year who have since become some of my closest dive buddies. We thought it would be a good chance to invite [rosa.xie_](https://www.instagram.com/rosa.xie_/), another friend from that Green Island trip whom they had not seen in a while, to dive with us.<br>
We spent a while deciding where to go until I remembered Rose Garden, a site I had heard about for years but none of us had visited.

## Getting There

Before setting out, I watched [Wei-Yi's video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gk6NjPrCEMY&t=269s). You can park at the same [first parking lot](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HA7Lm1PufmLYVjZv6) used for Elephant Trunk Rock, then walk in the opposite direction.<br>
The entrance looks more like a patch of overgrown grass than a trail, but once you pass through it, the entry point is only a short walk away.

- Access is straightforward, parking is easy, and the walk from the parking lot to the entry point takes less than three minutes.
- The main downside is that the shore is quite exposed, with almost no shade for changing in the summer heat. If you do not mind walking a little farther, you can change in the parking lot restroom first.

## The Dive

We entered the water at around 8:30 a.m. Most accounts online describe Rose Garden as murky, but visibility along the Northeast Coast had been quite good lately, and we had around ten meters that day.<br>
Setting the float line was also easier than expected, unlike Chaojing, where coral covers so much of the bottom that it can be difficult to find anywhere to secure it.

There is no need to worry about missing the rose coral. It covers a vast area, with patches of other colorful corals scattered throughout.<br>

## Final Thoughts

Pros:

- Parking is easy, and the walk to the entry point takes less than three minutes.
- There are plenty of shaded spots on shore for leaving sandals, and getting into the water is about as easy as at Longdong Site No. 4.
- The rose coral covers a large area and appears within two hundred meters, so there is no need to search for it.
- The depth is as approachable as Bitoujiao, but the water here is much more open. It is also better suited to portraits with coral, since the coral at Bitoujiao is too shallow for the same compositions.
- There are plenty of stir-fry restaurants and seafood eateries nearby for lunch.

Cons:

- For a shower, you will need to drive out and ask one of the nearby scuba shops.
- By the time you get out of the water, the nearby stir-fry and seafood restaurants usually have long lines, so eating elsewhere may be easier.

We actually got out of the water at around 11 a.m. Much like Chaojing, the current tends to pick up at around ten, and visibility begins to drop.

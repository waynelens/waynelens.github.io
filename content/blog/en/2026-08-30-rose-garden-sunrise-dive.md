## Introduction

I had occasionally talked with my dive buddies about doing a sunrise dive, but actually making it happen was harder than it sounded.

- We had to wake up long before sunrise to allow time for the drive and our pre-dive preparations.
- We would be entering the water almost entirely in the dark, so choosing a suitable entry point required extra care.

## The Shuanghe Crew

It turned out that the friends I usually dived with along the Northeast Coast were every bit as willing to do something ridiculous as I was.<br>
On the final weekend before I left for Green Island, [bedbedrpre](https://www.instagram.com/bedbedrpre/), [7p_hhh](https://www.instagram.com/7p_hhh/), and [bear81511](https://www.instagram.com/bear81511/) all agreed to join me for a sunrise dive at Rose Garden.

After taking a red-eye flight from Cologne together on our previous trip, we once again had to switch quickly from a full day of work into a middle-of-the-night departure.<br>
Fortunately, we all lived around Shuanghe, which at least made meeting up a little easier.

## Rose Garden

After diving at Rose Garden for the first time earlier that month, I felt that its entry point was relatively manageable and less risky in the dark.

When we reached the nearby parking lot, we found that plenty of nighttime fishing boats were still operating there. The harbor was actually livelier at night than in the morning.<br>
Having people and lights around helped ease some of the tension of entering the water in the dark.

Once we reached the entry point, however, the shore break was no joke.<br>
Honestly, I wanted to call off the dive. Everyone watched the waves for a moment and then entered one after another anyway.<br>
Using our flashlights to keep track of each other, we eventually managed to kick clear of the shore.

### Watching the Sunrise from the Sea

We already knew the water would be almost completely dark, so we did not rush to dive down. Instead, we spent most of the time holding on to the float, chatting, and watching the sky shift from deep blue into the first light of morning.
